## 2. Search Intent and Keyword Foundation

### Primary intent

The main SEO intent is **local commercial investigation with transactional follow-through**. A parent searches for activity options, compares suitability and trust signals, then wants to enquire about dates, age groups, fees, and availability.

Secondary intents are:

- **Informational:** finding ideas for children’s activities, outdoor learning, camps, or creative workshops in Kathmandu.
- **Navigational:** finding Young Explorers Academy, its Google listing, Instagram profile, contact information, or location.
- **Transactional:** requesting programme availability, fees, registration information, or a visit.

## 3. Authority and Link-Building Strategy

The goal is not to collect the largest number of links. The goal is to earn relevant references from Kathmandu education, parenting, events, culture, community, and local-business sources.

### 3.1 Digital PR: create a linkable local resource

Create one useful, original asset each quarter. The strongest initial asset is:

**“Kathmandu Children’s Activities Report: What Parents Want From Camps and Workshops”**

Step by step:

1. Create a short, transparent survey for Kathmandu parents. Ask about children’s age bands, preferred activity types, preferred days, transport concerns, budget questions, outdoor-learning interest, and information parents need before registering.
2. Collect responses through the academy’s parent community and clearly label the sample size, dates, methodology, and limitations.
3. Do not publish children’s names, faces, contact details, or sensitive family information without documented consent.
4. Publish the findings as a webpage with quotable statistics, simple charts, a methodology section, and a downloadable summary.
5. Add a short expert interpretation from the academy, without turning the report into an advertisement.
6. Pitch the findings to Kathmandu local media, parenting publications, education writers, event calendars, community newsletters, and relevant NGOs.
7. Offer ready-to-use numbers, a short quote, the original report URL, and permission to republish a chart with attribution.
8. Promote the report through Instagram and partner organisations, then track referring domains and referral enquiries.

Potential PR angles:

- What Kathmandu parents look for before choosing a children’s camp
- Why parents value outdoor learning beyond the classroom
- The most requested creative and communication activities for children
- How families plan children’s activities during school holidays

Never manufacture survey results or present academy customer feedback as representative of all Kathmandu families.

### 3.2 Digital PR: event and community stories

For each future public event:

1. Create a dedicated event page with the date, venue, age guidance, registration instructions, organiser, and contact details.
2. Give the event a clear media angle, such as children’s public speaking, creative learning, outdoor discovery, or family participation.
3. Prepare a press pack with a short release, approved photographs, captions, organiser quote, and contact person.
4. Pitch before the event to Kathmandu Post, local culture and events desks, neighbourhood publications, school-community newsletters, and relevant event calendars.
5. After the event, publish a factual recap with original photographs and link to the next relevant programme page.
6. Ask partners and venue hosts to link to the official event page, not only to an Instagram post.

Kids Carnival 2025 can be used as historical credibility, but it must remain labelled as a past event.

### 3.3 Skyscraper technique for local activity content

Use the technique to improve useful local information, not to copy competitors.

1. Search Google for terms such as “kids activities in Kathmandu,” “holiday camps Kathmandu,” and “outdoor activities for kids Kathmandu.”
2. Record the strongest local pages in a spreadsheet: URL, topic, age information, date freshness, practical details, backlinks, and missing information.
3. Identify gaps parents still need answered: age suitability, Baluwatar location, dates, fees in NPR, what to bring, transport, meals, accessibility, contact process, and whether an event is past or upcoming.
4. Build a materially better guide, for example: **“The Parent’s Guide to Choosing Children’s Activities in Kathmandu.”**
5. Include a comparison framework, questions to ask providers, a planning checklist, and verified local examples. Keep Young Explorers Academy as one clearly disclosed example, not the sole subject of an apparently neutral guide.
6. Add original photographs, expert commentary, and a useful downloadable checklist.
7. Contact sites that linked to the weaker or outdated guide. Explain the specific improvement and suggest the new resource only where it genuinely helps their readers.
8. Refresh the guide annually and update the outreach list.

### 3.4 Broken link building

1. Find Kathmandu parenting, school, event, tourism, community, and education pages with dead links using a browser extension or backlink tool.
2. Prioritise broken links whose original topic matches children’s activities, camps, outdoor learning, creative learning, or family events.
3. Confirm that the academy has a live page that genuinely replaces the missing resource. If not, create the resource first.
4. Email the webmaster with the exact broken URL and location on their page.
5. Offer the relevant replacement URL without demanding a link.
6. Follow up once after 7 to 10 days. Stop after the second message.
7. Record the result and do not use automated mass outreach.

### 3.5 Relevant link opportunities

Prioritise these source types:

- Kathmandu parenting and family publications
- Nepal education and early-learning organisations
- Local event calendars and cultural listings
- Baluwatar and Kathmandu neighbourhood portals
- Community associations and resident newsletters
- Workshop partners such as storytellers, artists, and educators
- Venue and event-host pages
- Local newspapers and radio websites
- Charities or community projects with which the academy has a genuine relationship
- Parent bloggers who independently review or cover local activities

Avoid paid link networks, private blog networks, bulk directory blasts, spun guest posts, irrelevant international directories, and links purchased solely to manipulate rankings.

## 6. Reviews, Social Proof, and Community Trust

### 6.1 Ethical review workflow

1. Ask every family for honest feedback after a real programme or event, not only families who appear happy.
2. Send a direct Google review link or clear instructions at an appropriate follow-up moment.
3. Explain that the rating and wording are entirely their choice.
4. Never offer money, discounts, gifts, or preferential treatment for a positive review.
5. Never write reviews for parents, ask staff to pose as customers, or gate unhappy families away from Google.
6. Reply to positive and negative reviews with specific, respectful, non-sensitive responses.
7. Do not reveal a child’s personal information in a public response.
8. Track review count, rating trend, recurring themes, and response rate monthly.

Suggested follow-up message:

> Thank you for joining Young Explorers Academy. If you have a moment, we would appreciate an honest review of your experience on Google. Your feedback helps other Kathmandu families understand our camps and activities. Please share whatever feels accurate, whether positive or constructive: [Google review link]

### 6.2 Use existing proof responsibly

The site already has three public Google review excerpts covering holiday-camp enthusiasm, summer activity variety, caring teachers, safety, and flexible timing. Continue to:

- Link excerpts to the public Google profile.
- Keep reviewer names and quotations accurate.
- Date-stamp the rating snapshot when used.
- Add new reviews only after checking that they are public and authentic.
- Turn recurring themes into service improvements and future content, not exaggerated claims.

### 6.3 Community and influencer partnerships

Build trust through relevant local people and organisations, not generic follower counts.

Potential partners:

- Kathmandu parenting creators with an authentic local audience
- Children’s authors, storytellers, artists, and activity facilitators
- Family photographers who already serve Kathmandu families
- Neighbourhood associations in Baluwatar and nearby areas
- Schools and parent communities, subject to their promotion policies
- Community event organisers and cultural organisations
- Outdoor, nature, and child-wellbeing initiatives

Partnership process:

1. Make a shortlist based on audience fit, local relevance, engagement quality, and child-safety standards.
2. Offer a clearly defined collaboration: a co-hosted workshop, useful parent guide, community event, or behind-the-scenes learning feature.
3. Agree in writing on dates, deliverables, disclosures, image consent, child safeguarding, and links.
4. Ask partners to link to the most relevant official page when they publish coverage.
5. Label paid or gifted collaborations clearly.
6. Measure referral visits, enquiries, registrations, earned links, and qualified local reach.
7. Reuse the resulting material only with written permission.

Do not ask creators to stage children’s experiences, publish identifiable children without consent, or make safety and outcome claims they cannot verify.

## 7. Measurement and Reporting

Create a monthly off-page dashboard with:

- New referring domains, grouped by relevance and authority
- Local citations created, corrected, or removed
- Google Business Profile views, calls, website clicks, direction requests, and messages
- Search Console impressions and clicks for Kathmandu keyword groups
- Branded search impressions for Young Explorers Academy
- Referral sessions and enquiry conversions by source
- Number of authentic reviews, average rating, and response rate
- Digital PR pitches, placements, mentions, and earned links
- Partner traffic and qualified enquiries
- Cost per qualified enquiry for any paid partnership

Quality checks matter more than raw link count. A relevant Kathmandu publication or trusted education partner is more valuable than dozens of unrelated directory links.
